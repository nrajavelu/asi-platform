Question one

Where does strategic analysis actually break down?

Macro and micro never meet.

The external environment and the internal scorecard are graded as two separate exercises, so the link between "why" and "so what" is never actually tested.

Internal and external get confused.

A weak internal process and an imposed external cost end up filed as the same kind of threat — because nothing forces the distinction.

The feedback loop is the bottleneck, not the thinking.

A grading queue, not the difficulty of the material, sets the pace of the course.

Question two

What does an AI-augmented strategy room change?

It reads the filing, not a summary of it.

Every factor is pulled from the actual source document — with the page it came from attached.

It draws the line, then asks the student to defend it.

A macro trend gets connected to a specific internal number live, in front of the student — not handed over as a finished answer.

It teaches by asking, not telling.

A follow-up question on the weakest part of the reasoning, in the moment — the way a professor would, at the scale a professor can't.

The engine underneath

One system decides how much thinking a question deserves.

The Aizentify AI Gateway sits between every question and every answer. It doesn't treat "tag this paragraph" and "defend this classification" the same way — and nothing reaches a student until it's been checked against the source.

Quick check

Tagging a factor, matching a citation — a fast, inexpensive pass, run on every document.

Deep reasoning

The actual analytical work — connecting a macro trend to a specific number, holding the Socratic dialogue.

Expert review

A faculty appeal or a grading dispute — the most careful read the system has available, reserved for when it matters most.

Nothing is ever guessed: every claim is checked against the actual source before it's shown. And because the platform isn't locked to one piece of underlying technology, quality keeps improving without the classroom experience changing underneath it.

Data governance

Every answer is grounded in something faculty put there — nothing a student wrote.

The knowledge base

Faculty-provided source filings — 10-Ks, transcripts, policy briefs. Chunked and indexed the moment they're added; the only material the Gateway is ever allowed to cite.

The activity record

Every student hypothesis, every Socratic turn, every score. Logged and timestamped, but never mixed into the source material a case is graded against.

Faculty provides the source documents and the rubric.

Entered at case setup — or added mid-term, when a fresher filing should replace or supplement the original.

Students provide their hypothesis and every turn of the dialogue.

Captured continuously, as they work — never treated as a source, only as a record of their own reasoning.

The system logs the scores and the citation checks.

Generated on every submission, attributed, and retained alongside the conversation that produced them.

Both stores are kept for the course's life and beyond — reviewable by faculty or the institution at any time, not just at grading.

Rigor that matches the standard

This isn't a shortcut around the framework. It enforces it.

THE TEST

"Can the institution control it directly?" is applied before every classification — not offered as a suggestion, required before a submission is accepted.

PRIORITY

Every Opportunity and Threat carries how much it matters and how likely it is — the same triage a strategy consultant runs before recommending action.

EVIDENCE

Every claim traces to a page, a paragraph, a line. Nothing is accepted on the system's word alone — including its own inferences, which are labeled as such.

Why this wins

Not a faster worksheet. A different standard of proof.

01

Grounded, not generated

A generic AI wrapper answers from memory and sounds confident either way. Every claim here traces to a page, a paragraph, a line — or it's labeled an inference and never treated as fact.

02

Teaches judgment, not answers

It challenges the weakest part of a student's reasoning instead of handing over a finished classification — the discipline a professor teaches, at a scale one professor can't reach alone.

03

Enforces the standard, doesn't imitate it

The internal/external test and the impact-and-likelihood triage aren't decoration — they're required before a submission is accepted, the same way a rigorous case discussion would insist on them.

04

Economical by design

The AI Gateway matches the depth of reasoning to the stakes of the question, so a full semester section runs for the cost of a few textbooks — modeled, auditable, and never a surprise.

Traditional case teaching tests whether a student can fill a grid. The ASI Platform tests whether their reasoning survives being questioned — and does it for every student, on every case, the moment the work is submitted.
