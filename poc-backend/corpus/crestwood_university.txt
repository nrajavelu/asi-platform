Crestwood University — Enrollment Strategy Case (illustrative sample corpus, not a real filing)

Crestwood University's incoming first-time degree-seeking class declined 9.1% from
the prior academic year, according to the university's IPEDS enrollment filing.
This is the fourth consecutive year of decline. The filing attributes the trend
to "continued softness in the regional 18-year-old population," citing a state
demographic office projection showing the college-age cohort shrinking through
the current decade across the university's primary recruitment region.

The university's endowment covers 6% of the annual operating budget, compared to
a median of 34% among AACSB-accredited private university peers cited in
Crestwood's own accreditation self-study draft. The self-study describes
Crestwood as "more tuition-dependent than 82% of institutions in our peer
comparison set."

Crestwood's online MBA program, launched eighteen months ago, has grown to 340
enrolled students with a 71% completion rate, compared to a 58% sector average
for online graduate business programs reported in the program's Year 2 report.
The report credits a redesigned advising model introduced at program launch.

The IPEDS filing presents the 9.1% enrollment decline as a single figure without
a peer-institution comparison. The filing does not include data showing whether
Crestwood's decline rate over the same period was larger, smaller, or
comparable to other private universities of similar size in the same
recruitment region.

The Year 2 online MBA report notes that 22% of newly enrolled online MBA students
this year previously inquired about, but did not enroll in, an on-campus
graduate program at Crestwood. The report does not state whether this
represents net-new demand for the university or a shift of demand from one
Crestwood program to another.
