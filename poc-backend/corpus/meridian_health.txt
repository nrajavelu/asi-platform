Meridian Health — Regulatory & Market Entry Case (illustrative sample corpus, not a real filing)

Meridian Health's remote cardiac monitoring platform received FDA breakthrough-device
designation eighteen months ago. The designation grants an expedited review pathway
for the device and priority engagement with FDA staff during the clearance process,
but it does not by itself set or guarantee any specific reimbursement rate.

A proposed CMS rule, published in the Federal Register, would reduce reimbursement
for standard telehealth office visits (billing codes 99213 through 99215) by 12%
starting next fiscal year. The proposed rule text describes the reduction as
applying to "telehealth office visits billed under the standard evaluation and
management code set" and does not, in the sections reviewed for this case, contain
a separate rate schedule for remote monitoring devices with breakthrough-device
status.

Meridian's investor update for the most recent quarter discloses a patient
retention rate of 68%, compared to a sector average of 79% reported by the same
industry benchmarking service Meridian cites elsewhere in the same document.

Company leadership stated on the earnings call that they are "confident the
breakthrough designation will support favorable payer conversations," but the
call transcript does not reference any completed or pending negotiation with CMS
regarding a device-specific reimbursement code separate from the standard
telehealth visit codes affected by the proposed rule.

An industry newsletter cited in Meridian's investor relations materials notes that
some breakthrough-designated devices have historically qualified for separate
Medicare billing codes months or years after designation, following a distinct
administrative process from the standard telehealth rulemaking described above.
The newsletter does not state whether Meridian's device is currently in that
process.
