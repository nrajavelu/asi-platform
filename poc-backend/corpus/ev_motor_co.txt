EV Motor Co. — Q2 Strategy Case (illustrative sample corpus, not a real filing)

EV Motor Co. faces a 15% tariff increase on lithium imports due to new trade policies
enacted in the second quarter. Management disclosed the schedule takes effect at the
start of the third fiscal quarter and applies to lithium carbonate and lithium
hydroxide imports sourced from its two primary overseas suppliers.

The company's internal battery manufacturing yield is 82%, below the 91% industry
benchmark cited in its most recent investor update. Yield is defined as the share of
battery cells produced that pass final quality inspection without rework. Management
attributes the gap to a legacy production line that has not yet been retrofitted with
the newer thermal-calibration equipment used on the company's second and third lines.

However, EV Motor Co. holds 42 proprietary patents in solid-state battery technology,
concentrated in electrolyte stability and thermal management. Twelve of the forty-two
patents were granted in the last eighteen months. Two peer manufacturers have
publicly disclosed licensing inquiries into adjacent patent families, though no
agreements have been signed as of this filing.

Separately, the company's direct-to-consumer order backlog grew 18% quarter over
quarter, which management attributes to broader consumer sentiment toward electric
vehicles rather than any specific product change. A regional sales director noted in
the earnings call that lead times on the entry-level trim have extended from six to
nine weeks as a result.

The trade policy brief accompanying the tariff announcement notes that a narrow
exemption exists for imports used exclusively in domestic solid-state battery
research and pilot-line production, subject to an annual volume cap the company has
not yet requested. Whether EV Motor Co.'s pilot line qualifies under that exemption
has not been confirmed by counsel as of this case's writing.
